# {{< style class="rune" >}}abte{{< /style >}}

The rolling meadows were punctuated by jagged breaks of crumbling rocks, connected by small stretches of scraggly woods. As the sun sank below the grass, casting a soft, warm glow, A* sought shelter in the nook of a rock formation. He settled his rucksack on the ground and took stock of his provisions, ensuring his provisions would last until H&. He kindled a tiny fire, and heated up some salted cod and dried bread. Letting the flickering light warm up his face, he dozed off with his forearm propping up his head.

The night air swept across the hills, funneling through the river valley K& before spilling out into the open sea. At the edge of this channel stood old fisherman F*'s shack. When he was young, he had built it here to take advantage of the morning winds and tides when heading out to sea, and coming in for the late afternoon. After ensuring his schooner was tied down for the upcoming storm, he hobbled up the dock to his hut, put the day's catch on the fish smoker, and fell asleep to rhythmic creaks of the boards.

A* was not asleep for long before he startled awake at the pattering of hooves. The fire had gone out, yet the moon adequately illuminated the figure dismounting his saddle. He was of medium stature, with a scruffy beard. His horse carried a lumpy saddlebag, covered by an embroidered wool cloth. The stranger carried a blade, though the tip was fashioned in a curve that A* had not seen before, and nor did he recognize the patterns on the tunic and dark crimson patches on the stranger's leather boots.

{{< softbreak >}}

"Names B*", he growled, stretching out his hand. When A* didn't reciprocate, the man drawled "Are you a party of one?"

{{< softbreak >}}

Unsure of how to respond A* sputtered out, "Err no, my companions are riding ahead on a hunt. Hopefully we should meet back up by dawn." Though he had no partners and the last thing he saw was a measly squirrel, it did not feel wise for A* to reveal his singularity. The man was already quite strange, and his unpronounceable name further added to A*'s suspicion.

{{< softbreak >}}

"May I join yer here till the morn?"

{{< softbreak >}}

At this suggestion, A* quickly got to his feet, avoiding the unwanted invitation. "Ah, see, I was about to set off, I have wasted enough time as it is. Why so eager to stop?"

{{< softbreak >}}

"My horse is tired, not much further I can go."

{{< softbreak >}}

"How far have you travelled?"

{{< softbreak >}}

At this, the man mumbled something which A* could not make out. "Where are you and your party heading?" continued the stranger, "I would like to hurry to the nearest town."

{{< softbreak >}}

Furrowing his brow at the response, A* headed off. The pair walked along, and the morning fog rolled into the hills and the sky began to slowly brighten. Though he had not encountered many people in his life, A* decided that, compared to the occasional trader in his village, B*'s mannerisms were out of the ordinary, and he was not to be trusted. Questioning B* did not yield much, the stranger would not tell him anything. The most he learned was that the stranger had to depart for his current trip incredibly quickly, and for some unknown reason, desperately needed to find the nearest town.

{{< softbreak >}}

Suddenly A* dropped to fix his shoe, motioning for the stranger to continue on without him. Standing up after removing the annoying pebble, he looked at the stranger from behind for the first time. He noticed that there were bumps visible through the strangers' tunic. Ever so slightly that only the keenest eye could notice, it was as though there were small bones protruding from his back, hidden under his skin, and his tunic. Catching up hastily to avoid suspicion, A* could not help but thinking that he had imagined it. Nonetheless, he could not shake the image from his mind.

{{< softbreak >}}

The stranger broke his train of thought. "Where are ye from, anyway?"

{{< softbreak >}}

"I've grown up my entire life in a small village, around a week's worth of travel from here. I just turned 16, so I left to go look for an apprenticeship and work. I'm hoping I can find something in K& up ahead."

{{< softbreak >}}

"Aha! So you too, indeed, are looking for a nearby town."

{{< softbreak >}}

"Yes, I guess so." responded A* disappointedly, since he had now given the stranger a reason to stick with him the whole way.

{{< softbreak >}}

And so they continued on the path, passing through creeks and thickets, in valleys and hills. Hunger taking hold, they decided to break for lunch. A* started a fire, thankfully B* disappeared into the bushes, giving A* room to think. 

{{< softbreak >}}

Who could this man be? Given his accent, embroidered clothes, and strange sword, he was obviously not local, at least to the villages A* had encountered. He had heard stories of wanderers, but nothing specific. His boots, on second thought, were strangely familiar. He had a similar pair at home, kept for slaughtering pigs. They, too, were stained a dark crimson, though A* had never given much thought to it. Before he could dwell on it, B* came crashing back through the undergrowth, insisting they be off.

{{< softbreak >}}

"Argh, enough waiting! Let's make haste!" A* started packing up camp, but he did not finish before B* started complaining; "Ugh, is there any way we can go faster?"

{{< softbreak >}}

"You have a horse, you can ride ahead, if you'd like."

{{< softbreak >}}

"Indeed, but I have no way to know which way I shall go." The stranger turned, "Tell you what, why don't you ride with me? We'll reach town in only a day."

{{< softbreak >}}

"I'm fine walking, thank you." A* said curtly.

{{< softbreak >}}

"Two gold pieces, then."

{{< softbreak >}}

A* looked at him. "For what?"

{{< softbreak >}}

"For your company, and for showing me the way."

{{< softbreak >}}

A* considered the offer. It was more than a fair price for a day's company, enough to share a saddle. His family rarely had much coin to spare, exchanging whatever they earned for goods and necessities as soon as they could. He wasn't accustomed to having money of his own, and the prospect of earning some was appealing.

{{< softbreak >}}

B* hauled A* up behind him with a single, effortless pull, and before A* could find his balance, the horse surged forward. A* had never ridden anything; the sudden, violent rhythm of even the horses quick trot knocked the breath straight out of his lungs. With no stirrups of his own, his knees slipped against the horse’s slick flanks. He was forced to grab fistfuls of B*’s embroidered coat just to keep from pitching backward into the dirt. Right there, inches from his own shin, B*’s crimson-stained boot bounced rhythmically against the stirrup, and his strange swords pressed against A*'s thigh. A* decided to keep his mouth shut, avoiding a confrontation, but also to control his gagging as the horse switched between a trot and canter.

{{< softbreak >}}

After what seemed like forever, the walls of K& finally came into view. Instead of riding through the main gate, B* jerked the horse into a side ditch and dismounted from the saddle.

{{< softbreak >}}

"Here ya go." He barked, and 2 heavy coins fell into A*'s palm. Before he could say anything, B* was gone.

{{< softbreak >}}

A* turned the coins over in his hand, feeling the grooves of the metal. Looking closely, he noticed the gold was engraved with a serpent like animal, which he had never seen. Even though he rarely held gold, he knew that the coins he had just received were not the standard pieces handled in his village. Pocketing the gold, he climbed out of the ditch and approached the front gate.

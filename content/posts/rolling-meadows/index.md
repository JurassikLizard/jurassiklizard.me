# {{< style class="rune" >}}abte{{< /style >}}

The rolling meadows were punctured by jagged breaks of crumbling rocks, connected by small stretches of scraggly woods. As the sun drifted down below the grass and ignited the shimmering air with a soft warm glow, A* took shelter in the nook of a rock formation. Sitting just on the edge of the woods and meadows, he settled his rucksack on the ground and took stock of his provisions. After ensuring his provisions would last until H&, he kindled a tiny fire, heating up some salted cod and dried bread. Letting the flickering light warm up his face, he dozed off; his forearm propping up his head

The night air swept across the hills, funneling through the river valley K& before spilling out into the open sea. At the edge of this channel stood old fisherman B*'s shack. When he was young he had built it here to take advantage of the morning winds and tides when heading out to sea, and coming in for the late afternoon. After ensuring his schooner was tied down for the upcoming storm, he hobbled up the dock to his hut, put the day's catch on the fish smoker, and fell asleep to rhythmic creaks of the boards.

A* was not asleep for long before he startled awake at the pattering of hooves. The fire had gone out, yet the moon adequately illuminated the figure dismounting his saddle. He was of medium stature, with a scruffy beard. His horse carried a lumpy saddlebag, covered by an embroidered wool cloth. The stranger carried a blade, though the tip was fashioned in a curve that A* had not seen before, and nor did he recognize the patterns on the tunic and dark crimson patches on the strangers leather boots.

{{< softbreak >}}

"Are you a party of one?" drawled the man.

{{< softbreak >}}

"No, my companions are riding ahead on a hunt. They should be back by dawn." Though he had no partners and the last thing he saw was a measly squirrel, it did not feel wise for A* to reveal his singularity.

{{< softbreak >}}

"May I join you here till the morn?"

{{< softbreak >}}

"Ah, I was about to set off, I have wasted enough time here as it is. Why so eager to stop?"

{{< softbreak >}}

"My horse is tired, not much further I can go."

{{< softbreak >}}

"How far have you travelled?"

{{< softbreak >}}

At this, the man mumbled something which A* could not make out. "Where are you and your party heading?" continued the stranger, "I would like to make haste to the nearest town."

{{< softbreak >}}

As the pair headed off, the morning fog rolled into the hills and the sky began to slowly brighten. Determining that the stranger was not to be trusted, A* wanted to question the stranger on their trip to H&. However much he pressed, though, the stranger would not tell him anything. The most he learned was that the stranger had to depart for his current trip incredibly quickly, and for some unknown reason, desperately needed to find the nearest town.

{{< softbreak >}}

Suddenly A* dropped to fix his shoe, motioning for the stranger to continue on without him. Standing up after removing the annoying pebble, he looked at the stranger from behind for the first time. He noticed that there were bumps visible through the strangers' tunic. Every so slight that only the keenest eye could notice, it was as though there were small bones protruding from his back, hidden under his skin, and his tunic. A* jogged to catch up with the stranger, and resolved himself start a new conversation, as to not get lost in thought about the bumps. 

{{< softbreak >}}

The stranger, though, beat him to it. "Where are yourself from, anyway?"

{{< softbreak >}}

"I've grown up my entire life in the small village of N&, around a weeks worth of travel from here. Just turned 16, so I left to go look for an apprenticeship and work. I'm hoping I can find something in K&."

{{< softbreak >}}

"Aha! So you too, indeed, are looking for a nearby town."

{{< softbreak >}}

"Yes, I guess so." responded A* disappointedly, since he had now given the stranger a reason to stick with him the whole way.

{{< softbreak >}}

And so they continued on the path, passing through creeks and thickets, in valleys and hills. Soon enough, the stranger started complaining; "Argh, is there any way we can go  faster?"

{{< softbreak >}}

"You have a horse, you can ride ahead, if you'd like."

{{< softbreak >}}

"Indeed, but I have no way to know which way I shall go."
"Tell you what, why don't you ride with me? We'll gallop there in only a day."

{{< softbreak >}}

"I'm fine walking, thank you." A* said curtly.

{{< softbreak >}}

"I'll pay you 2 gold to be my guide."

{{< softbreak >}}

This, of course, piqued A*'s interest. He'd never been able to handle money, as the few coins that his family earned were always quickly exchanged in a barter for goods. "Very well" he said. 

{{< softbreak >}}

And so the pair rode on, passing the countryside at a gallop rather than a walk. A*, who'd never been on a horse, had to hold on for dear life and did not have a chance to appreciate the scenery they were passing. There was not much opportunity for conversation, but A* did find out that the strangers name was B*.
